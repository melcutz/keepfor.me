Readwise Alternative: Calm Reading, Highlights, and Native MCP

Looking for a lighter, open-source alternative to Readwise? See how Keepfor.me handles distraction-free reading, character-anchored highlights, hybrid semantic search, and 46 MCP tools starting at $5/month.

Product Comparison Guide · November 2026 Back to Homepage

Readwise and Readwise Reader are widely known among heavy readers. Readwise's Full plan ($9.99/month billed annually or $12.99/month billed monthly, per readwise.io/pricing) bundles spaced-repetition highlight review and note-app sync (for Kindle, Obsidian, Notion, and Roam) with Readwise Reader for web articles, RSS feeds, newsletters, PDFs, and EPUBs. Readwise Lite ($5.59/month billed annually or $6.99/month billed monthly) covers highlight review and daily digests without the Reader app.

People who start looking for a Readwise alternative usually have one of four reasons: they want a calmer, focused web reader without an all-in-one RSS/EPUB/newsletter inbox, they want an open-source (AGPL-3.0-only) codebase they can self-host and audit, they want a lower monthly subscription ($5/month Personal or $9/month Pro), or they want their saved articles and highlights accessible directly inside Claude and Cursor via 46 MCP tools.

Calm reading, passage highlights, and dual snapshots

Keepfor focuses on four core jobs: capture, store, search, and share context with AI assistants. Every piece of the reading pipeline is built for speed and permanence:

  • Frictionless capture across devices: Save web articles in one tap with the Manifest V3 browser extension, JavaScript bookmarklet, Android native Web Share Target, or iOS Apple Shortcuts using your existing Safari session.
  • Distraction-free reader sanctuary: Articles are parsed with trafilatura to strip cookie banners, popups, and clutter, then rendered in Paper, Warm Sepia, or Night themes with Sans, Serif, or Mono typography.
  • Character-anchored highlights: Quote passages inside any saved article anchored by exact character offsets (start_offset and end_offset), attach personal notes and colors, and edit notes without corrupting the original excerpt.
  • Permanent dual HTML snapshots: Keepfor stores both the original raw HTML (raw.html) and sanitized reader HTML (clean.html) in Cloudflare R2 object storage so your reading list survives link rot.
  • Restorable 30-day Trash: Deleting an item moves it to Trash while keeping its database row, highlights, R2 snapshots, and vector embeddings intact for 30 days.

Your highlights and articles in Claude and Cursor (46 MCP tools)

Most reading apps treat highlights as something you export to a second app so you can search them later. Keepfor turns your reading library itself into an AI context vault:

  • Remote Streamable HTTP MCP server: Connect Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor, or opencode directly to https://app.keepfor.me/api/mcp using a Personal Access Token.
  • 46 MCP tools covering your whole vault: Search by passage (search_passages) or across items (search_library), create and list highlights (create_highlight, list_highlights, update_highlight, delete_highlight), read full articles with pagination (read_item), manage collections and hierarchical tags, or generate a weekly digest (library_digest).
  • Ground-truth citations: Passage search returns permanent snapshot URLs and character offsets so your AI assistant cites exact sources from your library instead of guessing.

Importing your Readwise CSV and existing bookmarks

Readwise lets you export your highlights and saved documents as a CSV file from readwise.io/export or from Reader's command palette. Keepfor Cloud's CSV importer reads Readwise CSV exports directly (matching Article URL, Book Title, Tags, and Document tags), deduplicates multiple highlight rows from the same article into a single saved link, and merges both article and highlight tags. Book quotes that have no web URL are skipped, and highlight text in the CSV is not imported as character-anchored article highlights. Keepfor also accepts Netscape Bookmark HTML files, zips of Markdown and text notes, Google Keep Takeout zips, and Evernote .enex archives, and exports your library in HTML, CSV, or JSON at any time.
