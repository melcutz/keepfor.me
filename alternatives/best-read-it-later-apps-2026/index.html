Best Read-It-Later Apps in 2026: What Survives and What Works

After Mozilla shut down Pocket in July 2025, choosing a read-it-later app became a question of survival, search, and AI access. Here is an honest look at the landscape in 2026.

Buyer's Guide · November 2026 Back to Homepage

When Mozilla shut down Pocket on July 8, 2025 (and deleted remaining accounts after the export window closed on November 12, 2025), longtime Pocket readers had to find a new home for their reading lists. That shutdown changed how people evaluate a read-it-later tool. A clean font and a browser button are no longer enough; you need to know whether your archive can survive the company behind it, and whether you can actually find and use what you saved months later.

Five rules for picking a read-it-later app in 2026

  1. Unconditional export in standard formats. You should be able to export your entire library as Netscape Bookmark HTML, CSV, or JSON at any time, without waiting for a shutdown announcement.
  2. Open source with a practical self-host option. Closed-source cloud services last as long as their parent company's priorities. An open-source license (such as AGPL-3.0-only) that you can run on your own account means nobody can pull the plug on your library.
  3. Permanent snapshots, not just bookmarks. Pages disappear and links rot. A real archive stores snapshots of the article HTML and clean extracted text, not just a list of URLs.
  4. Hybrid keyword plus semantic search. Once your library passes a few hundred items, exact keyword matching fails whenever you remember an idea ("that post about SQLite composite indexes") instead of the exact title. Combining full-text keyword search with vector semantic search solves the write-only archive problem.
  5. Direct AI assistant connectivity (MCP). In 2026, researchers and developers work inside Claude Desktop, Claude Code, and Cursor. Exposing your library over the Model Context Protocol (MCP) lets your assistant search, read, summarize, and organize your own saved sources with citations.

How the options break down

1. Keepfor.me: Best for AI Context Vault, Semantic Search, and Serverless Self-Hosting

Keepfor.me is built for developers, researchers, and collectors who want a calm distraction-free reader paired with an AI context vault. Under the hood, it runs on Cloudflare Python Workers, D1 SQLite with FTS5, R2 object storage (saving both raw.html and clean.html snapshots), and Cloudflare Vectorize semantic embeddings.

  • Why pick Keepfor: You want an open-source (AGPL-3.0-only) vault you can self-host for $0 on your own Cloudflare account, or a managed cloud plan where both Personal ($5/month or $55/year) and Pro ($9/month or $90/year) include hosted MCP (46 MCP tools) and hybrid semantic search. Personal includes 150 AI summaries and 50 ask-your-library queries per month; Pro scales to 1,000 AI summaries, 500 ask-your-library queries per month, and a priority extraction queue.
  • Where Keepfor is Honest About Tradeoffs: Keepfor is a fast web application and installable PWA with a browser extension, iOS Safari Shortcut, and Android Web Share Target, rather than native App Store desktop or mobile binaries.

2. Raindrop.io: Best Budget Bookmark Manager and Native Platform Apps

In continuous development since 2013, Raindrop.io is the most established visual bookmark manager in the category.

  • Why pick Raindrop.io: It has a generous permanent free tier, its Pro plan costs roughly $28/year (about half the price of Keepfor Personal at $55/year), and it ships polished native apps on Mac, Windows, Linux, iOS, and Android.
  • Where Raindrop.io differs from Keepfor: Both products ship official MCP servers (Raindrop's help docs note that Stella's features work inside ChatGPT and Claude via MCP). Raindrop is closed source with no self-hosting option, while Keepfor is open source (AGPL-3.0-only) on Cloudflare and exposes 46 MCP tools covering passage search, chunked article reading, highlights, and library cleanup. If your top priority is the lowest-cost cloud bookmark manager with native desktop and mobile apps, pick Raindrop.io.
